LOCKPICK LEARNER v0.7 - MEASURED AGAINST 275 REAL RECORDED FILES
================================================================

Everything here comes from the user's own recorded data: 50 human attempts
(264 probes, 9 wins) and 210 autonomous attempts (815 probes, 0 wins).


0. A CORRECTION TO v0.6
-----------------------
v0.6 claimed the v0.4/v0.5 SUCCESS template matcher never fires, based on a
numpy reimplementation of cv2's TM_CCOEFF_NORMED that scored 0.199/0.197 on
two real SUCCESS frames. The real logs disprove that: on the actual machine
the template scored 0.643-0.998 on all 9 human wins and never above 0.076 on
any of the 41 losses. The reimplementation was not faithful; the original
detector works. It is back, running alongside the band/arc test - either one
firing is a success, so both a template mismatch and a resolution change are
survivable.


1. WHY IT NEVER OPENED A LOCK: IT ONLY EVER SAW 4% OF THE LOCK
--------------------------------------------------------------
Measured over all 210 autonomous attempts:

  "ramp found" already in the first state       175/210  =  83%
  median share of the lock covered per attempt              4%
  attempts that covered more than half the lock          0/210
  moves chosen: 450x -0.040, 266x +0.040, 6x anything bigger

The cause is one number. The rotation detector works in 1-degree steps, so a
lock that is NOT moving still reads 0.022-0.089. wobble_threshold was 0.025 -
inside that noise. So the first probe of nearly every attempt declared a ramp,
the +/-0.04 micro-step clamp took over, and the pick never went anywhere. The
sweet spot was in the 96% it never visited.

  wobble_threshold  0.025 -> 0.10   (above the measured noise floor)
  a ramp now needs 2 agreeing probes, not 1


2. SEARCH BEATS LEARNING, BY 3-4x
---------------------------------
An attempt is about 6 probes (2.67 s / ~5 probes measured). Three search
strategies, 3000 simulated attempts each, same learning code:

  target half-width      0.015   0.025   0.040   0.060   0.100
  sweep + micro-step     13.6%   18.6%   20.3%   20.7%   20.7%
  coarse grid + halving  20.9%   34.3%   55.2%   70.6%   80.4%
  gradient estimate      27.7%   38.8%   56.6%   63.0%   64.4%

The coarse grid was tuned the same way: 4 cells is best for a 6-probe budget,
5-6 for 8 probes. So the search is now:

  1. probe 4 evenly spaced cells - cover the whole lock, no creeping
  2. from the best cell, step out and halve the step whenever a side stops
     paying (the model picks which side to try first - that part is learned)
  3. refine probes use the full finishing hold, because that is what actually
     opens the lock - still ONE press per probe, not two

Real human wins look exactly like this, not like micro-steps: 0.049 -> 0.195
-> 0.956 -> 0.942 in four probes.


3. DOES IT IMPROVE OVER TIME? PARTLY, AND IT PLATEAUS FAST
-----------------------------------------------------------
3000 attempts, success rate per 250-attempt window:

  target 0.025:  50.2 54.0 53.2 53.6 55.2 56.5 54.3 56.3 55.4 55.0 55.8 58.3
  target 0.040:  68.8 72.8 73.4 68.9 64.5 69.5 71.4 71.3 68.0 73.2 70.2 71.0
  target 0.060:  84.2 86.6 84.6 81.9 85.0 85.4 86.6 85.8 87.0 82.6 86.4 83.8

Almost all of the performance is there inside the first 250 attempts, and
after that the curve is flat. The ceiling is set by the search structure, not
by training time. Anyone expecting it to keep climbing for thousands of
attempts will be disappointed - that is not what the numbers show.

Before this version, on the same simulator: 22.2% and 19.2%. Now: 52.5% and
80.9%. That gain is structure, not training.


4. WHAT IS ACTUALLY WORTH LEARNING
----------------------------------
The distance from the ramp to the opening point. The search always sweeps
left to right, so the first cell that answers is systematically LEFT of the
target - and the median correction is learnable. Measured:

  jump enabled                        55.0%   71.4%   82.9%
  jump disabled                       37.4%   55.2%   74.0%
  jump disabled, 3 coarse cells       37.9%   57.4%   69.2%
  jump disabled, 2 coarse cells       36.9%   52.6%   68.7%

So it is not merely "stop searching sooner" - the offset carries real
information. The user's own 9 wins give +0.363 as the median; simulated play
independently produced +0.393.


5. SHIPPED PRE-TRAINED POLICY
-----------------------------
esitreenattu_malli.json is copied into data\ on first run:

  demo_prior   28 states from the 50 real human attempts
               (+141 positive and -31 negative examples)
  Q            195 states from 12000 simulated self-play attempts across five
               target widths, so it is not tuned to one lock type
  offsets      the 3 usable ramp -> opening distances from the real wins

Delete data\model.json to go back to it, or just delete the bundled file to
start from nothing.


6. HONEST LIMITS
----------------
- The simulator's lock model is fitted to the recorded response falloff, but
  how close you must be for the lock to actually OPEN cannot be recovered from
  the recordings (the stored response is only the peak of the first 145 ms of
  a press). That is why every result above is reported across a sweep of
  target widths instead of one number.
- The Q part of the shipped model comes from that simulator. The demo_prior
  part comes from real play. If they ever disagree, the real data wins:
  run 2) TRAIN again after recording more of your own attempts.
- A faster probe cycle is the biggest untested lever: 6 probes -> 8 probes was
  worth about 10 percentage points in the tuning runs.
