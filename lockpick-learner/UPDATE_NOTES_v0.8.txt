LOCKPICK LEARNER v0.8 - THE PICK COULD NOT MOVE
================================================

Every earlier version, mine included, tuned the algorithm. The algorithm was
never the binding constraint.


1. THE PICK PHYSICALLY COULD NOT CROSS THE LOCK
------------------------------------------------
move_to() clipped every mouse step to +/-140 counts and allowed 8 of them.
The recorded calibration is 6000 counts across the lock. So:

  asked for   counts needed   what actually happened
    0.04           173        0.035  after 2 rounds  (~110 ms)
    0.12           518        0.118  after 6 rounds  (~330 ms)
    0.25          1080        0.187  after 8 rounds  (~440 ms)  <- truncated
    0.50          2160        0.187  after 8 rounds  (~440 ms)  <- truncated

The pick could never move more than 0.187 of the lock in one go, whatever was
asked for, and a big move burned 440 ms of a 3100 ms attempt. v0.7's coarse
grid asked for 0.25 jumps and silently got 0.187. That is why nothing got
better no matter what the search did.

It also explains the recorded move histogram exactly: 450x -0.040, 266x
+0.040, 6x anything larger. The small moves were the only ones that completed.

A move is now sent as a few back-to-back chunks of up to 900 counts with no
screen reads in between, then verified once or twice. A 0.25 move costs about
60 ms instead of 440 ms, and it actually arrives.


2. THE ROTATION DETECTOR WAS THE NOISE FLOOR
---------------------------------------------
The old detector scored 180 candidate lines through the lock with a python
loop and took the darkest. Its answer is quantised to whole degrees, and on
real frames it put a still lock at 0.089. That is where the "noise floor"
came from - not the game.

Replaced with the keyway measurement: take the dark pixels inside the
cylinder and compute the principal direction of that blob. On the same
frames:

                          old detector    keyway detector
  lock at rest (4 frames)     0.089        0.009 - 0.017
  lock turned  (3 frames)     varies       0.416 - 0.991
  cost per read              24.0 ms       3.4 ms

So the floor is 5x lower and a read is 7x cheaper. wobble_threshold drops
from 0.10 to 0.05 because a real but distant ramp can now be trusted.


3. WHAT THE TWO FIXES DO TOGETHER
----------------------------------
The probe cycle - move, press, read - goes from about 550-670 ms to about
170 ms, so an attempt is roughly 18 probes instead of 4.5. Same search code,
same learning code:

  target half-width         0.015   0.025   0.040   0.060   0.100
  v0.5/v0.6 behaviour           -   18.6%       -   22.0%       -
  v0.7 (6 probes)           36.9%   52.5%   69.2%   80.9%   92.6%
  v0.8 (18 probes)          52.9%   74.7%   79.3%   90.5%   96.6%

Recorded reality before any of this: 0 successes in 210 attempts.


4. WHAT IS STILL HONEST TO SAY
-------------------------------
- The two fixes above are measured directly, not simulated: the move clip is
  arithmetic on the recorded calibration, and the detector numbers come from
  real frames. Those are solid.
- The success percentages are simulator output. The lock model is fitted to
  the recorded response falloff, but how close you must be for the lock to
  actually OPEN cannot be recovered from the recordings, which is why every
  row is a sweep across target widths rather than one number.
- Learning still plateaus early. Nearly all of the gain above is structure -
  moves that arrive, reads that are honest, and enough probes to use them.
  Do not expect the curve to keep climbing for thousands of attempts.
